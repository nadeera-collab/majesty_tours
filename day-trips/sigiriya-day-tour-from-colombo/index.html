Overview

Sigiriya is a 5th-century rock fortress rising around 200 metres above the surrounding plain, with royal gardens, the famous Mirror Wall and ancient frescoes near its summit. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site and one of Sri Lanka's most recognisable landmarks. Nearby, the Dambulla Royal Cave Temple, also UNESCO-listed, holds a series of Buddhist cave shrines painted and sculpted over more than a thousand years. Both sites sit in the island's Cultural Triangle, roughly 170km from Colombo.

How the day runs

1

Early morning: Pickup in Colombo and the drive north

We collect you from your Colombo hotel, the airport or the cruise terminal well before dawn breaks. This is a long day, and an early start is what makes it comfortable. Sigiriya is roughly 170km away, about four hours by road.

  • Hotel, airport or port pickup
  • Expressway and the Kurunegala road north
  • Breakfast or a tea stop on the way
2

Mid-morning: Sigiriya Lion Rock

The climb comes first, while the air is still cool and the stairways are quieter. From the landscaped water gardens you pass the frescoes and the Mirror Wall on your way to the lion’s paws, then take the final stairways to the summit and the ruins of the palace.

  • Royal water gardens
  • The Sigiriya frescoes
  • The Mirror Wall
  • The lion’s paws and the summit stairways
  • The palace ruins and the view over the dry zone
3

Midday: Lunch

A traditional Sri Lankan rice and curry lunch near Sigiriya, included in the day and a welcome sit-down after the climb.

  • Traditional Sri Lankan rice and curry
4

Afternoon: Dambulla Royal Cave Temple

Twenty minutes down the road, five cave shrines sit under an overhanging rock face, their ceilings painted and their floors crowded with Buddha statues added by successive kings. Shoulders and knees covered, shoes off at the entrance.

  • Five cave shrines
  • Ceiling frescoes following the contours of the rock
  • Reclining and seated Buddha statues
  • The view back over the plain from the temple terrace
5

Evening: Return to Colombo

The drive back south, with a stop or two along the way, and a drop-off wherever you need it: your hotel, the airport or back at the ship.

Best time to go

The Cultural Triangle sits in the dry zone, so this trip runs year-round. January to April and July to August are the driest and clearest. Whatever the month, the climb is far more pleasant started early, which is why the day begins before sunrise.

Frequently asked questions

How long is the drive from Colombo to Sigiriya?

About four hours each way for the roughly 170km, using the expressway for the first stretch and the A-road north through Kurunegala after that. That is the reason for the pre-dawn start: it puts you on the rock before the midday heat.

Do I have to climb Sigiriya to enjoy the day?

No. The water gardens, the boulder gardens and the museum at the base are worth the visit on their own, and the view of the rock from Pidurangala nearby is the photograph most people are after. Tell your guide and the day is arranged around what you want to do.

What should I wear?

Shoes you can climb 1,200 steps in, and clothing that covers shoulders and knees for the Dambulla cave temple. Shoes and hats come off at the temple entrance, so socks are worth having on a hot day, because the stone gets warm.

Are the entrance fees included?

Yes. Both the Sigiriya and Dambulla entrance fees are included in the price, along with the vehicle, chauffeur guide, fuel, parking, tolls and lunch.

Can we add Polonnaruwa or a Minneriya safari to the same day?

Honestly, no. Not without making the day unpleasant. Sigiriya and Dambulla already fill it. If you want Polonnaruwa, Minneriya and Kandy as well, the 5-day Cultural Triangle private tour covers all of them at a sane pace.

Can this run as a cruise shore excursion?

Only on a long call. Sigiriya is four hours from Colombo each way, so the ship needs to be in port for the best part of a day. Send us your call and sail-away times and we will tell you honestly whether it fits.
